# The eight major landmarks

Jade Harbor's eight major landmarks give the city and surrounding landscape recognizable places to visit. Each is an original interpretation of Chinese architectural or landscape composition, not a one-to-one reconstruction of a real historical site.

| Landmark | What to explore |
|---|---|
| **Jade Palace** | Pass through the outer and inner gates, cross the ceremonial courts, and climb to the raised principal hall. Side halls and a rear hall complete the enclosed palace composition. |
| **Northern Ridge Wall** | Follow a long wall route along the ridge, pass its roofed guardrooms, and use the stepped entry to reach the wall walk. |
| **Terrace Excavations** | Visit two excavation areas, view the abstract terracotta formations from their rims, descend into the pits, and explore the research hall. |
| **Harbor Guild Beacon** | Approach the merchant tower from the quay and promenade, then explore its upper public decks. |
| **Cloudstep Sanctuary** | Follow the mountain trail through the gate and lower court to the stepped main hall, retaining terrace, and overlook. |
| **East Cliff Chambers** | Reach the cliff terrace and inspect two shallow carved chambers with abstract niches and a cliff-attached facade. |
| **Reed Moon Garden** | Walk the pond edge and stepped causeway around the pavilion, rock composition, and planted landscape. |
| **Western Lookout Pagoda** | Climb the compact three-tier pagoda using its interior half-step stairs to reach the upper public floor. |

## Getting around

The landmarks connect to the city's roads, promenades, heritage trails, and mountain paths. Some viewpoints and upper floors require climbing rather than approaching from ground level. The Travel Journal groups each destination with its district or surrounding region; see [Travel Journal](travel-journal.md) for the 18 available travel hubs.

## Design approach

The landmarks are built into the established world rather than placed in a separate map. Their approaches, public floors, and routes are intended to be explored on foot. Their forms are fictional Chinese-inspired interpretations; they are not presented as exact historical replicas or to-scale reconstructions.

The world remains editable. Player changes take precedence over generated structures, so a modified or obstructed approach may no longer match the original route.

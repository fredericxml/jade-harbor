# City density and architectural variety

Jade Harbor is designed as a **city-first voxel world**. The ten districts share one established map footprint, with connected streets, closely arranged buildings, markets, courtyards, canals, waterfronts, workyards, and public spaces.

The goal is a city that feels continuous and occupied—not a collection of isolated buildings separated by large empty fields.

## A dense, connected layout

Buildings fit into established parcels and street relationships. Roads, public courts, Panda Plaza, canals, waterfront access, and landmark approaches keep priority over ordinary infill. Districts retain different street patterns and uses, so density does not mean every area looks or functions the same.

## Variety you can see

Building differences come from physical forms and how they meet the street:

- distinct building massings and heights;
- gable, hip, layered, split, offset, pavilion, and terrace roof forms;
- different ridge directions and roof spans;
- varied facade patterns, window rhythms, shutters, screens, and timber details;
- shopfronts, arcades, recessed porches, gates, and canopies;
- courtyards, side wings, rear rises, corner towers, and workyard arrangements.

The same district can contain different building families while retaining a coherent local architectural identity. Variety is not achieved by emptying parcels, expanding the map, or changing only the building color.

## Streets with a purpose

Public-facing buildings, shop entries, courtyards, quays, and landmark approaches give residents places to work, visit, patrol, and meet. Streets are part of the playable city, not just empty space between building shells.

## Landscape around the metropolis

The world combines the city with rivers, harbor space, ridge corridors, cliffs, karst scenery, tea terraces, orchards, gardens, and open landscape. Generic wild forest is kept below five percent in the measured world envelope; managed planting and landmark gardens have their own roles.
